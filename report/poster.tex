\begin{alertblock}{Objectives}



  \begin{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Model robot control as a generative process}: Represent the motor policy as a conditional denoising diffusion model conditioned on observations.

    

    \item \textbf{Learn expressive action distributions}: Capture complex, multimodal behavior by estimating the score function of the action space.

    

    \item \textbf{Enable stable iterative action refinement}: Perform inference through stochastic Langevin dynamics steps to produce coherent, high-dimensional actions over time.



    \item \textbf{Use differnet architectures for training}: Train the model on different complex architectures, such convolutions and transformers.



    \item \textbf{Train differnet simulations}: Train the model on different simulations and see the results.

\end{itemize}



\end{alertblock}



  \begin{block}{Introduction}



    The paper introduces \textbf{Diffusion Policy}, a framework for generating robot behavior by modeling the control policy as a \emph{conditional denoising diffusion process}. Instead of directly predicting actions, the policy learns the \emph{score function} of the gradient field of the action distribution, allowing it to iteratively denoise latent action samples conditioned on visual observations. By progressively refining these samples, Diffusion Policy produces \textbf{smooth, coherent, and high-dimensional motor commands}, enabling robust performance across complex manipulation and visuomotor tasks.

\begin{block}{Theoretical background}



Implicit policies represent the action distribution using an 

\textbf{Energy-Based Model (EBM)}:

\[

p(\mathbf{a} \mid \mathbf{o}) = 

\frac{e^{-E_{\theta}(\mathbf{o}, \mathbf{a})}}{Z(\mathbf{o}, \theta)},

\]

where \( Z(\mathbf{o},\theta) \) is an intractable normalization constant

with respect to the action \(\mathbf{a}\).



In the diffusion formulation, instead of directly optimizing the energy 

function, the policy performs \emph{iterative denoising} of an initial noisy 

action sample. Starting from \( \mathbf{x}_K \sim \mathcal{N}(0, I) \), the 

diffusion model produces a sequence of progressively denoised actions

\[

\mathbf{x}K,\, \mathbf{x}{K-1},\, \dots,\, \mathbf{x}_1,\, \mathbf{x}_0,

\]

where \( \mathbf{x}_0 \) represents the final noise-free action conditioned on 

the observation.



Each denoising step follows the update rule

\[

\mathbf{x}_{k-1}

= 

\alpha_k \big( \mathbf{x}k - \gamma_k\, \varepsilon{\theta}(\mathbf{x}_k, k) \big) 

\;+\; \mathcal{N}(0,\, \sigma_k^2 I),

\]

where \( \varepsilon_{\theta} \) predicts the noise component at timestep \( k \), 

and \( \alpha_k, \gamma_k, \sigma_k \) are diffusion schedule parameters.



This iterative refinement mirrors the gradient-based update of an 

Energy-Based Model:

\[

\mathbf{x}' = \mathbf{x} - \eta\, \nabla E_{\theta}(\mathbf{x}),

\]

with the score function \( -\nabla_{\mathbf{a}} E_{\theta} \) approximated 

by the diffusion model's noise predictor \( \varepsilon_{\theta} \). Thus, the 

diffusion denoising steps can be viewed as performing implicit gradient 

descent in the learned energy landscape of actions.



    

\end{block}

  \begin{block}{Training and Inference}



    \textbf{(a) General formulation.} 

        At timestep \( t \), the policy receives the latest \( T_o \) observations 

        \( \mathbf{O}_t \) and predicts a sequence of \( T_a \) future actions 

        \( \mathbf{A}_t \). This defines a conditional action-generation process 

        based on recent visual and proprioceptive context.

        

        \vspace{0.4em}

        

        \textbf{(b) CNN-based Diffusion Policy.} 

        In the convolutional architecture, the observation encoding \( \mathbf{O}_t \) 

        conditions every convolutional layer through 

        \emph{FiLM} (Feature-wise Linear Modulation). Starting from a noisy action 

        sample \( \mathbf{A}^K_t \sim \mathcal{N}(0,I) \), the noise-prediction network 

        \( \varepsilon_{\theta} \) iteratively removes noise over \( K \) denoising         steps, 

        yielding the final denoised action sequence \( \mathbf{A}^0_t \).

        

        \vspace{0.4em}

        

        \textbf{(c) Transformer-based Diffusion Policy.} 

        In the transformer architecture, the embedding of \( \mathbf{O}_t \) is 

        injected into each decoder block via multi-head cross-attention. Action 

        embeddings use causal self-attention, meaning each action token attends only 

        to itself and earlier actions. This enforces autoregressive consistency while 

        conditioning the entire generation process on the current observation.
